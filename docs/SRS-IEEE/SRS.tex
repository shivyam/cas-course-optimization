\subsection{Purpose}

The CAS (Computing and Software) Department currently manages course outlines and assessment schedules on a 
course-by-course basis. Course instructors set their deadlines independently, so students in the same program 
and year can face busy weeks of midterms and deadlines with no department-level visibility into them. 
CAS administrative staff must also manually check each outline against McMaster course outline policy~\citep{McMasterUCMP2025} and 
the Canadian Engineering Accreditation Board (CEAB) requirements~\citep{CEAB2025}, and follow up with instructors when 
information is missing.
\\ \\
The CAS Deadline Management System is intended to make assessment dates visible across courses and to 
identify policy and accreditation gaps early. It reduces the manual effort of checking outlines against 
these requirements and helps avoid weeks with heavily clustered assessments. Instructors and administrators 
remain in the loop and responsible for every decision. The system only flags issues and makes suggestions, 
and never changes a course outline or assessment date on its own.

\subsection{Scope}
The CAS Deadline Management System aims to improve course outline review and assessment scheduling within 
the CAS Department at McMaster University. The system addresses the lack of 
department-wide visibility into assessment dates and the manual effort required to verify course outlines 
against university policies and CEAB requirements. The system will allow CAS administrators to upload course outlines, extract and store 
relevant course and assessment information, identify missing information and potential policy or accreditation 
gaps, and visualize assessment schedules across courses. It will also identify potential assessment conflicts 
and suggest alternative dates to help distribute assessments more evenly throughout the academic term. 
The system will support reviewing and exporting extracted information to facilitate administrative coordination.
\\ \\
The primary users are CAS administrators and course instructors, while students may benefit indirectly 
from improved assessment scheduling. The system is intended to reduce administrative workload, 
improve policy compliance, and support more balanced assessment schedules.
\\\\
The system will provide recommendations for review rather than make decisions independently. 
It will not automatically modify course outlines, change assessment dates, or override instructor decisions. 
Instructors retain authority over their course schedules, and administrators remain responsible for 
reviewing policy compliance and accreditation requirements. The system's recommendations will be based 
on the available course outline data, timetable information, and defined policies; it will not guarantee 
that all scheduling conflicts or compliance issues are identified.

\section{System Overview}

This section provides general information about the system.  It identifies the
problem, the vision, and the interfaces between the system and its environment.
This section also describes user characteristics and lists the system
constraints.

\subsection{Problem Statement}

The CAS Department at McMaster University currently manages 
course outlines and assessment schedules independently across courses. Without a centralized 
view of assessment dates, students in the same program and academic year may face multiple
midterms and assignment deadlines within short periods. CAS administrative staff must also 
manually review course outlines for compliance with university policies and CEAB requirements. 
Missing or incomplete information, such as midterm dates listed as ``TBD,'' 
creates additional work and requires repeated communication with instructors.
\\\\
These challenges contribute to uneven student workloads, increased academic stress, and 
significant administrative overhead. Furthermore, instructors retain authority over their 
course schedules, limiting administrators' ability to directly resolve scheduling conflicts.
\\\\
The CAS Deadline Management System aims to improve this process by centralizing assessment 
information, identifying potential scheduling conflicts and policy compliance gaps, and 
suggesting alternative assessment dates. This will help administrators coordinate schedules 
more efficiently, reduce manual review efforts, and support more balanced student workloads 
while preserving instructors' authority over their courses.


\subsection{Product Vision}

The CAS Deadline Management System will provide the CAS Department with a trusted,
department-wide foundation for collaborative assessment planning. It will turn
course-outline information into a consistent, reviewable source of scheduling
knowledge that helps instructors and administrators make informed decisions
together. The system is intended to improve coordination and transparency over
time, while keeping final responsibility for course schedules and policy
interpretation with the appropriate human decision-makers.
  
\subsection{System Context}

\begin{center}
 \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{../SRS-IEEE/SystemContext2.3.png}

The CAS Deadline Management System is a web-based application intended to support 
course outline review, assessment scheduling, and policy compliance within the CAS Department at 
McMaster University. CAS administrators and course instructors 
interact with the system to upload course outlines, review extracted information, view assessment 
schedules, and evaluate potential scheduling conflicts and compliance issues.
Figure~\ref{Fig_SystemContext} summarizes these interactions by showing the system boundary
and its relationships with instructors, CAS administrators, the LLM, and the database.
\\ \\
The system processes course outlines and relevant academic scheduling information to extract 
assessment details, identify missing or potentially non-compliant information, and recommend 
alternative assessment dates. Results are presented to users for review, while instructors and 
authorized administrators retain responsibility for final scheduling and compliance decisions.
\\ \\
The system is intended for academic administration and planning rather than mission-critical or 
safety-critical applications. Since extracted information and automated recommendations may be 
incomplete or inaccurate, users must review flagged issues and verify important results before 
making decisions.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{User Responsibilities:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Provide course outlines and relevant academic scheduling information.
        \item Ensure submitted documents are appropriate for review and that the information provided is sufficiently complete and accurate.
        \item Review extracted course and assessment information and correct errors where supported.
        \item Evaluate flagged scheduling conflicts, missing information, and potential policy compliance issues.
        \item Decide whether to accept or reject suggested assessment dates and determine any final changes to course schedules.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{\progname{} Responsibilities:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Accept and process supported course outline documents and scheduling information.
        \item Extract and organize relevant course and assessment details for review.
        \item Validate extracted data for missing, inconsistent, or incorrectly formatted values where possible.
        \item Present assessment schedules and identify potential workload conflicts.
        \item Flag potential gaps in course outline requirements and applicable accreditation criteria.
        \item Generate alternative assessment date recommendations with supporting explanations.
        \item Present results and warnings to users without automatically changing course outlines or assessment schedules.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{External System Responsibilities:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{LLM:} Process course outline content to support information extraction and, where applicable, the generation of explanations for scheduling recommendations.
        \item \textbf{Database:} Store and retrieve course information, extracted assessment details, review results, and scheduling information.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}



\subsection{User Characteristics} \label{SecUserCharacteristics}

The primary users of the CAS Deadline Management System are CAS administrators 
and course instructors at McMaster University. Users are expected to have basic 
computer skills, including navigating web applications and viewing course information. 
CAS administrators should be familiar with course outline reviews, academic scheduling, 
and applicable university policies. Course instructors should have knowledge of their own 
course outlines and assessment schedules. No programming or machine learning knowledge is 
required. Users should be able to review extracted information, interpret flagged issues 
and scheduling recommendations, and make informed decisions regarding their courses or 
administrative responsibilities.

\subsection{System Constraints}

The CAS Deadline Management System must operate within the technical and institutional 
constraints of McMaster University. The following constraints apply to the system:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Deployment Environment:} The system must be deployable within 
    the computing resources made available by McMaster University. The availability and 
    capacity of these resources may limit the models and processing workloads the system can support.

    \item \textbf{Data Sources:} The system must process course outlines provided in PDF format. 
    Variations in document structure, formatting, and scan quality may affect the accuracy of 
    information extraction.

    \item \textbf{Language Model Cost and Hosting:} The system must use an LLM that is
    available at no cost to the project. GitHub Copilot should be preferred where it
    satisfies the system's technical and institutional requirements. Otherwise, the
    system should prioritize locally hosted, open-weight language models to minimize
    reliance on external services. Model selection must account for available computing
    resources and applicable licensing restrictions.

    \item \textbf{Human Decision-Making:} The system must not automatically modify course outlines or 
    assessment dates. All scheduling changes and final compliance decisions remain the responsibility 
    of instructors and authorized administrators.

    \item \textbf{Policy and Accreditation Requirements:} Compliance checks must be based on documented 
    McMaster University policies~\citep{McMasterUCMP2025} and applicable CEAB requirements~\citep{CEAB2025}. The system must support human review 
    of flagged issues rather than treating automated results as definitive determinations.
\end{itemize}

\noindent \begin{itemize}
  
\item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum \label{SR-CAS-01}:] \textbf{Centralized Course Outline Submission:} 
CAS administrators need a centralized location to collect and review course outlines.

\item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum \label{SR-CAS-02}:] \textbf{Course Information Extraction:} 
CAS administrators need relevant information extracted from submitted course outlines, including course 
details, assignments and projects, marking schemes, and midterm dates where available, and need to be able

\item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum \label{SR-CAS-03}:] \textbf{McMaster Policy Review:} 
CAS administrators need support in identifying potential failures of course outlines to comply 
with McMaster University and CAS department policies.

\item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum \label{SR-CAS-04}:] \textbf{CEAB Accreditation Review:} 
CAS administrators need support in reviewing course outlines against CEAB accreditation 
requirements, including evaluating how each course addresses relevant CEAB graduate attributes.

\item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum \label{SR-REC-01}:] \textbf{Assessment Schedule Analysis:} 
Professors/instructors and CAS administrators need to identify periods with high concentrations 
of assessment and midterm deadlines across relevant CAS department courses and reassess 
the schedule when dates are updated.

\item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum \label{SR-REC-02}:] \textbf{Scheduling Recommendations:} 
Professors/instructors need recommendations for alternative midterm and assessment dates 
that may reduce deadline bottlenecks for students, while being able to make the final decision.

\item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum \label{SR-REC-03}:] \textbf{Instructor Decision-Making:}
Professors/instructors need to retain authority over whether to accept or reject scheduling recommendations.

\item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum \label{SR-REC-04}:] \textbf{Handling Incomplete Information:} 
Professors/instructors and CAS administrators need to be informed when missing or unreadable 
course outline information may affect the reliability of compliance reviews or scheduling recommendations.

\item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum \label{SR-REC-05}:] \textbf{Authorized Access:} 
CAS administrators and professors/instructors need access to the information and functions relevant 
to their roles, while assuring sensitive information is protected and restricted to authorized users.

\item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum \label{SR-REC-06}:] \textbf{Shared Assessment Calendar:} 
Professors/instructors and CAS administrators need access to a shared calendar displaying 
relevant course assessment dates.

\item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum \label{SR-REC-07}:] \textbf{Exam and Restricted-Period Conflicts:} 
Professors/instructors and CAS administrators need to identify potential conflicts between 
assessments, exam dates, and applicable restricted periods.

\end{itemize}

  \item The administrator selects the option to submit a course outline.
  \item The administrator selects and submits the course outline.
  \item The system receives the document and attempts to extract relevant course information.
  \item The system confirms that the course outline has been submitted successfully.
  \item The system makes the submitted outline available for information extraction and review.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent \textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
  \item If the submitted document cannot be read, the system informs the administrator that a readable document is required.
  \item If the submission fails, the system informs the administrator and allows them to try again.
  \item If relevant course information is missing, the system proceeds with the information that is available and identifies the missing information.
  \item If the Copilot or LLM service is unavailable, the system informs the administrator that automatic information extraction cannot be completed. The submitted course outline remains available, and the administrator can retry extraction when the service becomes available.
\end{itemize}

\noindent \textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item If submission succeeds, the course outline is stored and available for review, even if automatic information extraction could not be completed.
  \item If submission fails, the administrator is informed that the course outline was not submitted successfully.

\section{Functional Requirements}

\noindent The system shall support the following capabilities, grouped by feature area.

\subsection*{Outline Intake and Extraction}
\begin{itemize}
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Intake_Data}:] The system shall accept course outlines in PDF format submitted by a CAS administrator or professor.
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Extract_Assessment}:] The system shall extract each assessment's name, type, date, and weight from a course outline.
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Extract_LO}:] The system shall extract the learning outcomes in a course outline and the graduate attributes each addresses, whether the outline lists them by number, names them, or only describes them.
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Extract_Flag}:] The system shall flag any extracted field that is missing, including a date that is marked TBD.
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Extract_Review}:] The system shall require a user to review and confirm extracted data before it is used for the calendar, conflict detection, or compliance checking.
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Dates_Tentative}:] The system shall allow an instructor to enter and update tentative assessment dates for their own courses.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Calendar and Conflict Detection}
\begin{itemize}
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Calendar}:] The system shall display confirmed and tentative assessment dates in a shared calendar that can be filtered by course, year, and program, with tentative dates marked as such.
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Work_Cluster}:] The system shall identify weeks in which a student cohort has more assessments than a configurable threshold.
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Work_Policy}:] The system shall identify assessments scheduled in periods restricted by McMaster policy~\citep{McMasterUCMP2025}, such as final exam periods.
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Reanalyze}:] The system shall re-run conflict detection for the affected courses and cohorts whenever an assessment date is added or changed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Scheduling Suggestions}
\begin{itemize}
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Suggest_Generation}:] The system shall, on explicit request by the instructor, suggest alternative dates, for assessments involved in a detected conflict or listed as TBD.
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Suggest_NoAuto}:] The system shall not change any assessment date without the explicit approval of the responsible instructor.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Compliance Checking}
\begin{itemize}
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Comp_Policy}:] The system shall check each outline against the McMaster course outline policy rules, including required sections and assessment weights totalling 100\%, and report each finding~\citep{McMasterUCMP2025}.
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Comp_Grad}:] The system shall compare the graduate attribute numbers in an outline with the master course sheet and flag any that are missing or inconsistent.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Access and Export}
\begin{itemize}
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Access_Login}:] The system shall require users to log in before they can view or change any data.
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Access_Roles}:] The system shall allow administrators to manage data for all courses, and shall allow instructors to change data only for their own courses.
\item[FR\refstepcounter{reqnum}\thereqnum \label{FR_Export_CSV}:] The system shall allow an administrator to export extracted assessment data as a CSV file.
\end{itemize}

\section{Data Requirements}

\subsection{Major Data Entities}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{User:} an administrator or instructor. Both can submit outlines and confirm extracted data. Instructors also enter tentative dates, and request and approve date suggestions, for their own courses.
\item \textbf{Course:} a CAS course offering in a given term, taught by one instructor. A course may be cross-listed. SFWRENG 4X03, COMPSCI 3X03, and MECHTRON 3X03 are one course taught by one instructor, with a separate course code for each program. The outline and its assessments are stored once.
\item \textbf{CourseCode:} one listing of a course. Each code belongs to one course, and a course has one or more codes. The program and year level are derived from the code and are not entered separately.
\item \textbf{CourseOutline:} a submitted course outline PDF. Each outline belongs to one course, including when that course has several course codes.
\item \textbf{Assessment:} a graded component of a course (midterm, assignment, lab, etc.) with a weight and a date. The date is empty when it is missing or TBD, and an instructor can enter a tentative date.
\item \textbf{LearningOutcome:} a learning outcome stated in an outline, with any graduate attribute numbers listed beside it. A user reviews each outcome after extraction, and the review status records whether it is still extracted or has been confirmed.
\item \textbf{MasterCourseRecord:} a row of the master course sheet, listing the graduate attribute numbers expected for a course code. A cross-listed course can match more than one row, one for each of its codes.
\item \textbf{AcademicCalendarEntry:} a holiday, exam period, or other restricted date range.
\item \textbf{DateRecommendation:} a suggested alternative date for an assessment, with its explanation and decision.
\item \textbf{ComplianceFinding:} a problem found when checking an outline against McMaster policy or the master course sheet.
\end{itemize}

\noindent An instructor teaches one or more courses. Each course has one or more course codes and one or more outlines. Each outline contains many assessments and learning outcomes, stored once for the course rather than once per course code. Student cohorts are found by grouping course codes that share the same derived program and year level. Workload conflicts are calculated from the stored assessments when needed, so neither cohorts nor conflicts are stored separately.

\subsection{Privacy and Data Retention} \label{SecPrivacyDataRetention}

\begin{itemize}

\item[DR\refstepcounter{drnum}\thedrnum \label{DR_Confidentiality}:] Course outlines and the data extracted from them are generally public. The master course sheet and other department data may be confidential, and shall only be available to authorized CAS users.

\item[DR\refstepcounter{drnum}\thedrnum \label{DR_Hosting}:] The system shall use GitHub Copilot as its language model where it meets the system's requirements, and a locally hosted open-weight model on CAS infrastructure otherwise. Copilot is an external service, so only publicly available course outline information may be sent to it. Confidential department data and student personal information shall not be sent to Copilot or to any other external service. All other course and department data shall be stored and processed on CAS-hosted infrastructure.

\item[DR\refstepcounter{drnum}\thedrnum \label{DR_Access}:] Users shall only see and change the data their role allows.

\item[DR\refstepcounter{drnum}\thedrnum \label{DR_Retention}:] The retention period for outlines, extracted data, and findings shall be 10 years. The Undergraduate Course Management Policy requires that course outlines be retained for at least 10 years~\citep{McMasterUCMP2025}.

\end{itemize}

\section{Non-Functional Requirements}

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Accuracy}:]
\textbf{Accuracy:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The system shall achieve at least 95\% accuracy for extracted assessment dates and
    weights on a representative test dataset of course outlines.
    \item The system shall achieve at least 95\% recall for predefined missing or incomplete
    information in the same test dataset.
    \item The system shall identify at least 95\% of predefined policy and CEAB compliance
    issues and report the results in the Verification and Validation Plan.
    \item These thresholds align with the success measures in the Problem Statement and
    Goals document~\citep{TeamBVSH2026PS}.
\end{itemize}

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Usability}:]
\textbf{Usability:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The system shall allow the CAS administrators and course instructors described in
    Section~\ref{SecUserCharacteristics} to upload course outlines, review extracted information,
    view assessment schedules, and interpret flagged issues without programming or machine
    learning knowledge.
    \item At least 80\% of participants in representative usability testing shall complete
    each designated core task without assistance.
    \item The 80\% target is a project-defined validation threshold intended to demonstrate
    that the core workflows are usable by the intended non-technical users.
\end{itemize}

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Maintainability}:]
\textbf{Maintainability:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The system shall be organized into modular components for document processing,
    information extraction, scheduling analysis, and compliance rules.
    \item Changes to one of these components shall not require unrelated components to be
    rewritten.
    \item For representative maintenance tasks defined in the Verification and Validation
    Plan, the effort required to implement each change shall be documented and assessed
    against the original implementation effort for the affected component.
\end{itemize}

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Portability}:]
\textbf{Portability:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The system shall be deployable in the designated McMaster University hosting
    environment.
    \item The system shall be usable through Google Chrome 120 or later, Mozilla Firefox 121
    or later, and Safari 17 or later on supported desktop operating systems.
    \item The deployment and core workflow tests defined in the Verification and Validation
    Plan shall be executed in each supported browser environment. These browser versions
    define the project's verification baseline and may be updated for a future release.
\end{itemize}

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Installability}:]
\textbf{Installability and Deployability:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A new maintainer shall be able to deploy the system to the designated McMaster
    University hosting environment by following the documented installation and deployment
    instructions.
    \item The documented procedure shall identify required software, configuration values,
    environment variables, database setup, and service startup steps without relying on
    undocumented knowledge.
    \item The deployment shall be verified by a deployment test in the Verification and
    Validation Plan, including confirmation that the deployed system supports the core
    workflows.
\end{itemize}

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Performance}:]
\textbf{Performance:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Under the test environment and workload defined in the Verification and Validation
    Plan, the system shall display previously processed assessment calendar data within 3
    seconds for at least 95\% of requests.
    \item For a supported course outline of up to 30 pages, the system shall complete document
    processing and information extraction within 60 seconds for at least 90\% of test runs,
    excluding failures or delays caused by unavailable external dependencies.

\section{AI and Machine Learning Requirements}

\noindent The system extracts information from course outline PDFs in two steps. First, a Python PDF text extraction library reads the text from each PDF. Then a large language model (LLM) cleans up that text and organizes it into structured data. Where an outline does not list graduate attribute numbers or their exact wording, the LLM identifies the attributes from the learning outcome text. The attributes are then compared with the master course sheet by the system's own code, so the confidential master course sheet is never sent to the LLM. The date suggestions come from a constraint solver (CP-SAT), and the assessment weight check is ordinary deterministic code, so neither uses the LLM. The preferred LLM is GitHub Copilot, with a locally hosted open-weight model on CAS infrastructure as the fallback. The LLM is kept behind a replaceable interface, so the model can be changed without changing the rest of the system. Copilot is an external service, and the only model input that may be sent to it is publicly available course outline text (\drref{DR_Hosting}).

\subsection{Model Inputs}

\begin{itemize}
\item[AIR\refstepcounter{airnum}\theairnum \label{AIR_Input_Text}:] The model shall receive the text that the PDF extraction library produced from each course outline, rather than the PDF file itself, together with the CEAB graduate attribute definitions~\citep{CEAB2025}. The master course sheet shall not be part of the model input (\drref{DR_Confidentiality}, \drref{DR_Hosting}).
\item[AIR\refstepcounter{airnum}\theairnum \label{AIR_Input_Privacy}:] Model input shall be sent only to GitHub Copilot or to a model hosted on CAS-operated infrastructure (\drref{DR_Hosting}). Only publicly available course outline text may be sent to Copilot. No student personal information shall be used as model input.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Model Outputs}

\begin{itemize}
\item[AIR\refstepcounter{airnum}\theairnum \label{AIR_Output_Struct}:] The model shall organize the extracted outline text into structured data containing, for each assessment, its name, type, date, and weight, and for each learning outcome, its text, the graduate attributes it addresses, and whether each attribute was listed in the outline or identified by the model (\rref{FR_Extract_Assessment}, \rref{FR_Extract_LO}).
\item[AIR\refstepcounter{airnum}\theairnum \label{AIR_Output_Source}:] Every structured value shall include the passage of extracted text it was taken from, so a reviewer can check it without opening the original document.
\item[AIR\refstepcounter{airnum}\theairnum \label{AIR_Output_Missing}:] The model shall only use information present in the extracted text, and shall report a value as missing, including a date that is marked TBD or is too vague to resolve, instead of inventing one (\rref{FR_Extract_Flag}).
\item[AIR\refstepcounter{airnum}\theairnum \label{AIR_Output_Review}:] No model output shall be used for the calendar, conflict detection, or compliance results until a user has confirmed it (\rref{FR_Extract_Review}).
\end{itemize}

This section explains the major decisions in this document that are not
obvious. It also states the assumptions that the requirements rely on
(Section~\ref{sec_Assumptions}). Each item references the requirement,
constraint, or assumption it justifies. The sources are the information
session and meetings with the CAS supervisors, the project's Problem
Statement and Goals~\citep{TeamBVSH2026PS} and Development Plan~\citep{TeamBVSH2026DP}, McMaster University's Undergraduate Course
Management Policy~\citep{McMasterUCMP2025}, and the CEAB Accreditation Criteria
and Procedures~\citep{CEAB2025}.

\subsection{Assumptions} \label{sec_Assumptions}

The requirements in this document rely on the following assumptions. Each one
narrows the scope of the system. If an assumption stops holding, the
requirements that reference it must be revisited.

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\item[A\refstepcounter{asnum}\theasnum \label{A_CourseCode}:] \textbf{Program
  and year level come from the course code.} The CAS department provides the
  list of course codes it offers. The subject prefix of a course code
  identifies the program (for example, SFWRENG, COMPSCI, MECHTRON), and the
  first digit of the course number identifies the year level (for example,
  SFWRENG 2DA4 is a second-year course). A cohort is the set of courses that
  share a program and year level. This replaces timetable data, which the
  system does not use.

\item[A\refstepcounter{asnum}\theasnum \label{A_CrossListed}:]
\textbf{Cross-listed course codes refer to one course.} A course offered under
several codes (for example, SFWRENG 4X03, COMPSCI 3X03 and MECHTRON 3X03) has
one outline, one instructor and one set of assessments. Its assessments count
once toward each cohort that one of its codes belongs to. A single course can
therefore belong to cohorts in different years.

\item[A\refstepcounter{asnum}\theasnum \label{A_Electives}:] \textbf{All
  courses at the same year level belong to one cohort, including electives.}
  Elective enrolment data is not available, and the CAS department has not yet
  said how electives should be grouped. Treating all same-level courses as one
  cohort may flag clusters that no individual student experiences. This is a
  conservative choice: it may produce false positives, but it does not hide
  real clusters, and users review every flag. This assumption is provisional
  (\lcref{LC_CohortDefinition}).

\item[A\refstepcounter{asnum}\theasnum \label{A_OffCycle}:] \textbf{Students
  taking courses outside their year level are not modelled.} A student who
  takes a higher-level course early, or a course out of sequence, accepts the
  additional workload. This is the student's responsibility, not the
  department's.

\item[A\refstepcounter{asnum}\theasnum \label{A_PublicOutlines}:]
  \textbf{Course outlines are public documents.} Outlines must be available to
  students \citep{McMasterUCMP2025}, so their content can be sent to a
  language model service covered by McMaster's institutional agreement~\citep{McMasterCopilot}. Other
  data held by the system, such as compliance findings and tentative dates, is
  not public.

\item[A\refstepcounter{asnum}\theasnum \label{A_AcademicCalendar}:]
  \textbf{Academic calendar dates are available before analysis.} Term start
  and end dates, recesses, and examination periods for the term being analyzed
  are published by the university and entered into the system before
  workload analysis is run.

\item[A\refstepcounter{asnum}\theasnum \label{A_MasterSheet}:] \textbf{The
  department provides the expected graduate attributes for each course.} The
  master course sheet used by \rref{FR_Comp_Grad} is supplied and maintained
  by the CAS department. The system does not decide which graduate attributes
  a course should cover.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{System Constraints}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Language model cost and hosting.} The client does not want to pay
  for ongoing services, so the language model must be free to the project.
  GitHub Copilot is preferred because McMaster's agreement with Microsoft
  provides it at no cost with enterprise data protection, meaning prompts are
  not used to train models~\citep{McMasterCopilot}. A locally hosted open-weight model is the fallback
  if programmatic access to Copilot is not available. The final choice has not
  been made (\lcref{LC_LLMHosting}).

\item \textbf{PDF input only.} The CAS supervisors stated that outlines
  published in other formats, such as HTML, are converted to PDF before they
  are submitted. Supporting one format keeps the extraction pipeline simpler
  to build and test (\rref{FR_Intake_Data}).

\item \textbf{Human decision-making} (\srref{SR-REC-03},
  \rref{FR_Suggest_NoAuto}). Instructors have authority over course delivery,
  and CAS administrators are not authorized to reassign assessment dates. The
  CAS supervisors stated that the project must not change course content or
  dates. This is a constraint, not a preference: the university, not the team,
  decides who may change assessment dates.

\item \textbf{Compliance grounded in documented policy.} Compliance checks are
  limited to rules stated in the Undergraduate Course Management
  Policy~\citep{McMasterUCMP2025} and the CEAB Accreditation Criteria and
  Procedures~\citep{CEAB2025}.
  Rules the team inferred itself could flag outlines incorrectly and could not
  be justified to instructors.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Functional Requirements}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Extracted data must be confirmed before use}
  (\rref{FR_Extract_Review}, \srref{SR-CAS-02}, \srref{SR-REC-04}). Course
  outlines do not follow a common format, so extraction errors are expected. An
  incorrect date or weight would affect the calendar, workload analysis,
  suggestions, and compliance checks. Administrators need extracted information
  they can rely on (\srref{SR-CAS-02}), and users need to know when it may be
  unreliable (\srref{SR-REC-04}). A confirmation step meets both needs by
  stopping an error before it reaches those outputs. The Development
  Plan~\citep{TeamBVSH2026DP} identifies this semi-automated approach as the fallback if fully automated
  extraction is not accurate enough.

\item \textbf{Missing and TBD values are flagged, not guessed}
  (\rref{FR_Extract_Flag}, \srref{SR-REC-04}). The CAS supervisors identified
  outlines listing ``Midterm Date: TBD'' as a core problem. A guessed date
  would appear in the calendar as if it were real. Flagging the value makes the
  gap visible so the administrator can follow up once, instead of repeatedly.

\item \textbf{Instructors can enter tentative dates} (\rref{FR_Dates_Tentative}).
  Outlines are often written before every date is fixed, which is why TBD dates
  occur. Letting instructors enter a tentative date gives the workload analysis
  something to work with, and marking it as tentative in the calendar
  (\rref{FR_Calendar}) keeps it from being mistaken for a final date.

\item \textbf{Clusters use symbolic constants} (\rref{FR_Work_Cluster}).
  There is no agreed definition of how many assessments in how many days is
  too many. The window and threshold are therefore the symbolic constants
  \textit{CLUSTER\_\allowbreak{}WINDOW} and
  \textit{CLUSTER\_\allowbreak{}THRESHOLD} (Table~\ref{tab:Constants}), so they
  can be tuned with the CAS supervisors without changing the requirement
  (\lcref{LC_ClusterDefinition}).

\item \textbf{Restricted periods come from university policy}
(\rref{FR_Work_Policy}, \srref{SR-REC-07}). The Undergraduate Course Management
Policy forbids tests or examinations worth more than
\textit{BAN\_\allowbreak{}WEIGHT\_\allowbreak{}LIMIT} during the test and
examination ban period. That period covers the last
\textit{BAN\_\allowbreak{}CLASS\_\allowbreak{}DAYS} days of classes and the days
before final examinations begin \citep{McMasterUCMP2025}. Assessments may also
not be due during recesses \citep{McMasterUCMP2025}. These are the restricted
periods the system checks.

\item \textbf{Conflicts are re-checked automatically; suggestions are not}
  (\rref{FR_Reanalyze}, \rref{FR_Suggest_Generation}). Detecting conflicts only
  reports facts about the stored dates, so it is re-run whenever a date changes
  to keep the calendar current. Suggestions are produced only when an
  instructor asks for them. The CAS supervisors noted that instructors already
  find unrequested follow-up disruptive, and unprompted suggestions would add to
  it.

\item \textbf{Every suggestion comes with an explanation}
  (\rref{FR_Suggest_Generation}, \ucref{UC_PROF_03}). Instructors keep final
  authority, so a suggestion is only useful if the instructor can judge it. An
  explanation that compares the workload in the original and suggested weeks
  lets the instructor check the suggestion against the data. If the
  explanation cannot be generated, the suggested dates are not shown with a
  made-up reason (\ucref{UC_PROF_03}).

\item \textbf{Policy rules the compliance check uses} (\rref{FR_Comp_Policy},
  \srref{SR-CAS-03}). The rules are taken from the Undergraduate Course
  Management Policy~\citep{McMasterUCMP2025}:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item assessment weights must total \textit{WEIGHT\_\allowbreak{}TOTAL};
  \item without approval, no single assessment may be worth more than
    \textit{MAX\_\allowbreak{}SINGLE\_\allowbreak{}WEIGHT};
  \item students must receive feedback worth at least
    \textit{EARLY\_\allowbreak{}FEEDBACK\_\allowbreak{}L2} (Level II) or
    \textit{EARLY\_\allowbreak{}FEEDBACK\_\allowbreak{}L3} (Level III and
    above) before the last day to cancel a course.
  \end{itemize} Weights that do not total
  \textit{WEIGHT\_\allowbreak{}TOTAL} are either an outline error or an
  extraction error, and both need review.

\item \textbf{Access is limited by role} (\rref{FR_Access_Login},
  \rref{FR_Access_Roles}, \srref{SR-REC-05}). Instructors may change only their
  own courses because they hold authority over those courses and no others.
  Administrators review outlines across the department, so they need access to
  all courses.

\item \textbf{Export as CSV} (\rref{FR_Export_CSV}). The CAS supervisors asked
  that stored data be exportable. CSV opens in the spreadsheet tools CAS staff
  already use and does not tie the data to this system. Other formats may be
  added later (\lcref{LC_ExportFormat}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Nonfunctional Requirements}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Accuracy targets} (\nfrref{NFR_Accuracy}). The Problem
  Statement and Goals~\citep{TeamBVSH2026PS} set extraction accuracy, missing-field recall and
  compliance detection at 90\%. The team raised
  \textit{EXTRACT\_\allowbreak{}ACCURACY}, \textit{FLAG\_\allowbreak{}RECALL}
  and \textit{COMPLIANCE\_\allowbreak{}RECALL} to 95\% while writing this
  document. Every extracted value is confirmed by a person before use
  (\rref{FR_Extract_Review}), so the system is only useful if review is mostly
  confirmation rather than correction. At 90\%, an administrator would correct
  roughly one value in ten, which would leave much of the manual effort the
  system is meant to remove. The targets are below 100\% because review remains
  the safeguard. The Problem Statement and Goals~\citep{TeamBVSH2026PS} will be updated to match.

\item \textbf{Maintainability is measured against likely changes}
  (\nfrref{NFR_Maintainability}). Section~\ref{sec_LikelyChanges} lists the
  changes the team expects. Measuring maintainability as the effort to make one
  of those changes, relative to \textit{FRACTION} of the original development
  effort, gives a target that can be tested rather than a general statement.

\item \textbf{Performance and usability targets} (\nfrref{NFR_Performance},
  \nfrref{NFR_Usability}). \textit{CALENDAR\_\allowbreak{}LOAD\_\allowbreak{}TIME}
  applies to stored data that users browse interactively, so it is short.
  \textit{EXTRACT\_\allowbreak{}TIME\_\allowbreak{}LIMIT} is longer because
  extraction runs once per outline when it is submitted, and the administrator
  reviews the result afterwards. These values, together with
  \textit{TASK\_\allowbreak{}COMPLETION}, are initial targets set by the team.
  They will be validated in the Proof of Concept and revised if they prove
  unrealistic.

\item \textbf{Installability} (\nfrref{NFR_Installability}). The system is
  expected to outlive the capstone team, and will be maintained by CAS IT staff
  or a later student team. Deployment therefore cannot depend on knowledge that
  only the current team holds, so every step must be documented and tested.

\item \textbf{Accessibility} (\nfrref{NFR_Accessibility}). The Accessibility
  for Ontarians with Disabilities Act~\citep{AODA2005} applies to McMaster's web content, and its
  standard for websites is WCAG 2.0 Level AA~\citep{IASR2011, WCAG20}. This is a legal requirement, not a
  design preference, so it is stated as a nonfunctional requirement that must be
  verified.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data Requirements}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{One record per cross-listed course} (\asref{A_CrossListed}).
  Storing a cross-listed course once means a correction made under one course
  code applies under all of them. It also prevents the same assessment from
  being counted twice in one cohort's workload.

\item \textbf{No student personal data.} Cohorts are derived from course codes
  (\asref{A_CourseCode}), so the system never needs to know which students
  take which courses. This reduces privacy risk and the amount of confidential
  data the system holds.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{AI and Machine Learning}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Suggested dates are computed by a deterministic solver, not
  generated by the language model} (\rref{FR_Suggest_Generation}). A language
  model can produce plausible but incorrect dates. A solver that works directly
  from the stored constraints produces dates that can be checked, and gives the
  same result for the same input. The language model is used to extract
  information from outlines (\airref{AIR_Output_Struct}) and to explain
  suggestions in plain language. It never computes suggested dates or checks
  assessment weights, which are done by deterministic code.

\item \textbf{The master course sheet is never sent to the language model}
  (\airref{AIR_Input_Text}, \drref{DR_Hosting}). The sheet is confidential
  department data (\drref{DR_Confidentiality}), and Copilot is an external
  service. Outlines word graduate attributes in different ways, so where the
  numbers or exact wording are missing, the model identifies the attributes from
  the learning outcome text (\rref{FR_Extract_LO}). It needs only that public
  text and the public CEAB attribute definitions to do so, not the sheet.
  Comparing the identified attributes with the sheet (\rref{FR_Comp_Grad}) is
  then a lookup that ordinary code can do exactly. Attributes identified by the
  model are less certain than ones listed in the outline, so they are reviewed
  like every other extracted value (\rref{FR_Extract_Review},
  \airref{AIR_Output_Review}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Likely Changes} \label{sec_LikelyChanges}

The following changes are expected during the life of the system. The design
should hide each of them, so that a change affects as few components as
possible. Table~\ref{tab:LC2Rtm} lists the requirements each change affects.

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_LLMHosting}:]
  \textbf{Language model and its hosting.} Copilot under McMaster's agreement~\citep{McMasterCopilot}
  is preferred, and a locally hosted model is the fallback. Either choice may
  change, and models will be replaced as better ones are released.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_OutlineFormat}:]
  \textbf{Course outline formats.} Instructors use different layouts. The
  university may also adopt a new outline template or syllabus platform, or
  the system may need to accept formats other than PDF.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_PolicyRules}:]
  \textbf{McMaster course management policy rules.} The Undergraduate Course
  Management Policy has been revised several times, most recently in 2025
  \citep{McMasterUCMP2025}. Its required outline contents, weight limits, and
  restricted periods may change again.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_AccreditationCriteria}:]
  \textbf{Accreditation criteria and the master course sheet.} The CEAB
  revises its criteria periodically \citep{CEAB2025}. The department may also
  change which graduate attributes each course is expected to cover.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_GAInference}:]
  \textbf{How graduate attributes are identified in an outline.} The system
  uses the attribute numbers or names in the outline where they are given, and
  the language model identifies them from the learning outcome text where they
  are not. This may change, for example if the department adopts an outline
  template that always lists the attribute numbers, or if the CEAB revises the
  attributes themselves \citep{CEAB2025}.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_ClusterDefinition}:]
  \textbf{Definition of a workload cluster.} The values of
  \textit{CLUSTER\_\allowbreak{}WINDOW} and
  \textit{CLUSTER\_\allowbreak{}THRESHOLD} are initial estimates. Assessment
  types may also be weighted differently (for example, a midterm counting more
  than a quiz).

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_CohortDefinition}:]
  \textbf{Definition of a cohort.} How elective courses are grouped
  (\asref{A_Electives}) is provisional and will be clarified with the CAS
  department.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_AcademicCalendar}:]
  \textbf{Academic calendar dates.} Term dates, recesses, and examination
  periods change every year.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_SchedulingMethod}:]
  \textbf{Method for generating suggested dates.} The Development
  Plan~\citep{TeamBVSH2026DP} proposes a constraint solver, with a simpler scoring method as a fallback.
  The method may change as the Proof of Concept shows what works.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_Notification}:]
  \textbf{How results reach instructors.} Email notification is on hold until
  it is confirmed with the CAS supervisors. Results may later be delivered by
  email or another channel.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_ExportFormat}:]
  \textbf{Export formats.} CSV is the only export format. Others, such as
  Excel or calendar files, may be requested.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_Authentication}:]
  \textbf{User authentication.} The system may move to McMaster's single
  sign-on if it is hosted on university infrastructure.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_Mosaic}:] \textbf{Use of
  enrolment or timetable data.} Integration with Mosaic is a stretch goal. It
  would replace course-code cohorts (\asref{A_CourseCode}) with cohorts based
  on actual enrolment. Historical MSAF data, if made available, could also be
  used to identify weeks with high past assessment pressure.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_ScopeExpansion}:]
  \textbf{Scope of courses covered.} The project began as part of a larger
  initiative covering both undergraduate and graduate CAS programs. The
  system may later include graduate courses or other departments.

\end{itemize}

% ==============================================================
\section{Unlikely Changes} \label{sec_UnlikelyChanges}

The design may assume that the following will not change. Each item gives the
reason it is considered stable.

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\theulcnum\label{ULC_InstructorAuthority}:]
  \textbf{Instructors keep final authority over assessment dates}
  (\srref{SR-REC-03}, \rref{FR_Suggest_NoAuto}). Under the Undergraduate
  Course Management Policy, instructors organize and present their courses, and
  changes to how a course is evaluated are made in consultation with the
  Department Chair~\citep{McMasterUCMP2025}. CAS administrators do not have the
  authority to change an instructor's assessment dates, and a software system
  cannot be given authority that the people using it do not have.

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\theulcnum\label{ULC_OutlineInput}:]
  \textbf{Course outlines remain the main source of assessment information}
  (\srref{SR-CAS-01}). University policy requires every course to have an
  outline that lists its assessments, weights, and due dates
  \citep{McMasterUCMP2025}. The file format may change
  (\lcref{LC_OutlineFormat}), but the outline itself will remain.

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\theulcnum\label{ULC_CourseCodeConvention}:]
  \textbf{The first digit of a course number gives its year level}
  (\asref{A_CourseCode}). The Undergraduate Calendar defines course numbers
  this way for every McMaster course: the first digit gives the level of the
  course~\citep{McMasterUGCal2025}. A change would affect every program and
  system at the university.

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\theulcnum\label{ULC_Roles}:] \textbf{The two
  user roles: CAS administrator and instructor} (\srref{SR-REC-05},
  \rref{FR_Access_Roles}). These roles reflect how outline review is divided in
  the department: administrators review outlines, and instructors own their
  courses. No other users will be given access.

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\theulcnum\label{ULC_AssessmentTerm}:]
  \textbf{Every assessment belongs to one course offering.} Academic
  records at McMaster are organized by course offering, and the course
  management policy is written at that level.

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\theulcnum\label{ULC_WebDelivery}:]
  \textbf{The system is used through a web browser}
  (\nfrref{NFR_Portability}, \nfrref{NFR_Accessibility}). Administrators and instructors use different
  devices and cannot be expected to install software. The Problem Statement and Goals~\citep{TeamBVSH2026PS}
  commits to a browser-based system with no additional hardware or software.

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\theulcnum\label{ULC_FirstYear}:]
  \textbf{Level I courses are excluded.} First-year engineering is common to
  all programs and is not run by the CAS department. This would change only
  if the Faculty's first-year structure changed.

\end{itemize}
