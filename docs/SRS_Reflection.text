\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{What went well while writing this deliverable?}

  We believe that the strongest part of the process was making useful progress
  by reviewing the document in small pull requests. Our team members carefully
  reviewed each section and highlighted duplicated descriptions, vague browser
  support, missing accessibility and deployment requirements, and missing or
  broken links. A team member also pointed out that the system-context figure,
  which was initially included without an explanation or legend, needed text
  explaining the relationships shown in the diagram. We added that explanation
  so the figure communicates system context rather than appearing to be
  included only to satisfy a documentation requirement.


  \item \textbf{What pain points did you experience during this deliverable, and
  how did you resolve them?}

  The main difficulty was converting a broad project vision into requirements
  that were specific enough to verify without pretending that uncertain design
  decisions had already been made. At first, we described the LLM, calendar,
  and recommendation features in terms of capabilities, but not in terms of
  failure behavior or evidence of success. Choosing numerical targets was also
  difficult. We addressed this by reviewing comparable document-processing and
  scheduling systems, consulting course requirements and project guidance, and
  discussing whether each target was realistic for our expected workload and
  resources.

  Another pain point was maintaining consistency across several documents. A
  safety roadmap initially used identifiers that were not the labels in the
  Safety and Security Requirements table, and some SRS sections repeated
  information instead of referencing it. We resolved this through our PR reviews.

  \item \textbf{How many of your requirements were inspired by speaking to your
  client(s) or their proxies?}

  The core requirements came from the project brief and conversations with the
  CAS project supervisors (Matt \& Connie): outline submission, extraction of assessment
  information, compliance checking, shared calendar views, workload-conflict
  detection, and instructor control over suggested dates.


  \item \textbf{Which courses have helped, or will help, your team to be
  successful with the capstone project?}

  Our Software Requirements course helped us distinguish stakeholder needs
  from functional and non-functional requirements. The course also helped us
  realize that statements such as ``balance workloads'' are not testable until
  they are converted into a measurable
  recommendation outcome. Our Software Testing course helped us define
  representative datasets, invalid-input cases, and verification methods. Our
  Database course provided the foundation for storing course and assessment
  information and will help us plan the structure of that information. Machine
  learning courses will be relevant to evaluating extraction quality, handling
  model uncertainty, and designing human review around LLM output.

  \item \textbf{What knowledge and skills will the team collectively need to
  acquire?}
Collectively, we need to build up our skills in a few areas to bring the 
different parts of the system together. This includes learning how to integrate 
an LLM, get consistent outputs from it, and handle issues like timeouts or 
unavailable services while keeping data private. We also need to figure out how 
to process course outline PDFs, including scanned documents, and turn the extracted 
information into assessment records that users can review. For scheduling, 
we need to learn how to use a constraint solver like CP-SAT to find assessment 
conflicts, account for restricted dates, and suggest better ways to spread out 
workloads without automatically changing dates. On the full-stack side, 
we need experience with database design, API development, authentication, 
and connecting the frontend to the different backend components. We also need 
to make sure the interface is accessible and test it against WCAG guidelines. 
Finally, we need to learn how to deploy and configure the system properly so 
that someone outside our team can set it up and maintain it.

  \item \textbf{How will the team acquire this knowledge and master these
  skills?}

The team will develop these skills through independent research,
official documentation, tutorials, and hands-on experimentation.
For unfamiliar technologies or tasks, we will conduct technical spikes 
(which we havea already started doing when evaluating llm models and testing our PDF text extraction libraries) to test
different approaches, identify limitations, and determine the best
solutions before implementation. We will document the findings of each spike on
\href{https://github.com/shivyam/cas-course-optimization/discussions}{GitHub Discussions}
so that the whole team can refer back to them throughout development.
We will also share knowledge through regular meetings and code reviews,
and continue learning through implementation, troubleshooting, and testing
as the project progresses.

\end{enumerate}
