\section{Introduction}

%\wss{You can include your definition of what a hazard is here.}

A hazard is a system condition or failure that could contribute to an undesirable outcome, resulting in losses 
like inaccurate accreditation assessments, poor deadline recommendations, a loss of course information, or sensitive data breaches.\\

The Course Deadline Management system is intended to support McMaster University's Computing and Software Department 
in reviewing Software Engineering course outlines for compliance with applicable university policies and Canadian 
Engineering Accreditation Board (CEAB) requirements. The system also analyzes course schedules to recommend assignment 
and midterm deadlines that reduce overlapping student workloads, while leaving final scheduling decisions to instructors.\\

This document identifies potential hazards associated with the Course Deadline Management system, examines their consequences, 
and establishes a basis for developing safety and security requirements to mitigate these risks.\\


\section{Scope and Purpose of Hazard Analysis}

%\wss{You should say what \textbf{loss} could be incurred because of the hazards.}

The purpose of this hazard analysis is to identify potential failures in the Course Deadline 
Management system that could negatively affect accreditation compliance reviews, course scheduling, 
or the handling of course information. The analysis covers the processing of course outlines, 
evaluation against McMaster policies and CEAB requirements, deadline conflict detection, and the 
generation of scheduling recommendations. The scope is limited to Software Engineering courses 
within the CAS department and excludes first-year courses, courses outside the department, 
and the security of the hosting infrastructure itself.\\

\noindent Potential losses resulting from system hazards include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Accreditation non-compliance, resulting in unaddressed violations of McMaster policies or CEAB requirements.
    \item Increased student workload conflicts due to overlapping assignment and midterm deadlines, potentially contributing to academic stress and more MSAF requests.
    \item Incorrect scheduling decisions resulting from inaccurate or misleading recommendations.
    \item Loss or corruption of course information, resulting in inaccurate course outline data and scheduling issues.
    \item Unauthorized disclosure of confidential information, including the master course sheet, compliance findings, and tentative assessment dates.
    \item Wasted administrator and instructor time due to false compliance findings, incorrectly identified deadline conflicts, or inaccurate recommendations.
    \item Loss of trust in the system due to repeated inaccurate or unreliable results, potentially leading users to stop using the system and reducing its intended benefits.
\end{itemize}


\section{System Boundaries and Components}

%\wss{Dividing the system into components will help you brainstorm the hazards.
%You shouldn't do a full design of the components, just get a feel for the major
%ones. For projects that involve hardware, the components will typically include
%each individual piece of hardware. If your software will have a database, or an
%important library, these are also potential components.}

The CAS Course Deadline Management system processes Software Engineering course outlines to support accreditation 
compliance reviews and deadline optimization. Its boundary includes the components responsible for processing course 
information, evaluating policy and accreditation requirements, detecting scheduling conflicts, generating scheduling 
suggestions, and managing stored information. The system uses course outlines, a master course sheet for graduate 
attribute checks, and the academic calendar to identify restricted scheduling periods. The LLM service, hosting 
infrastructure, users' web browsers, and instructors who prepare course outlines are outside the system boundary, 
but their interactions with the system and the information they provide are considered in the hazard analysis.\\


\noindent \textbf{C1 User Interface:} Allows CAS administrators and instructors to interact with the system, upload course outlines, and review compliance findings and scheduling recommendations.\\

\noindent \textbf{C2 Course Information Database:} Stores course outline information, extracted assessment details, compliance findings, and tentative assessment dates.\\

\noindent \textbf{C3 PDF Text Extraction:} Extracts text from uploaded PDF course outlines using a text-extraction library.\\

\noindent \textbf{C4 LLM Information Structuring:} Uses an external LLM service to interpret extracted text and structure relevant information, including marking schemes, assignment deadlines, and midterm dates.\\

\noindent \textbf{C5 Accreditation Compliance Analysis:} Evaluates course outline information against applicable McMaster policies and CEAB requirements, including graduate attribute checks using the master course sheet.\\

\noindent \textbf{C6 Conflict Detection:} Analyzes assessment dates across courses to identify overlapping deadlines and potential student workload bottlenecks, taking restricted scheduling periods into account where applicable.\\

\noindent \textbf{C7 Scheduling Suggestions:} Uses a CP-SAT solver to generate proposed assessment dates based on scheduling constraints, identified conflicts, and applicable restricted periods.\\

\noindent \textbf{C8 Results Presentation:} Presents compliance findings, detected conflicts, and proposed scheduling changes to administrators and instructors for review.\\

\noindent \textbf{C9 Authentication and Access Control:} Authenticates users and enforces permissions to restrict access to system functions and confidential information.\\

\noindent \textbf{C10 Data Export:} Exports permitted course information, compliance findings, and scheduling results for authorized users.\\

\noindent \textbf{C11 External Dependencies:} Includes external services and software dependencies, specifically the LLM service used to structure extracted course outline information. The hosting infrastructure and users' web browsers are also external to the system boundary.\\


\section{Critical Assumptions}

%\wss{These assumptions that are made about the software or system. You should
%minimize the number of assumptions that remove potential hazards. For instance,
%you could assume a part will never fail, but it is generally better to include
%this potential failure mode.}

The hazard analysis is based on the following assumptions about the system's operating environment, users, 
and input data. These assumptions establish the context for the FMEA, while risks arising when they do 
not hold will be assessed and appropriate mitigation measures identified.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hosting Infrastructure:} The hosting infrastructure is secured, backed up, and maintained by CAS IT. The security of the hosting infrastructure itself is outside the scope of this analysis.

\item \textbf{User Accounts:} Users access the system using valid McMaster accounts and do not share their credentials.

\item \textbf{Master Course Sheet:} The department-provided master course sheet contains correct information for the graduate attribute checks, as assumed in the SRS.

\item \textbf{Academic Calendar:} The academic calendar dates entered into the system are correct for the term being analyzed.

\item \textbf{Course Outline Confidentiality:} Course outlines are treated as public documents, consistent with the SRS. Other stored information, including the master course sheet, compliance findings, and tentative assessment dates, may be confidential.

\item \textbf{Policy and Accreditation Requirements:} The McMaster policies and CEAB requirements maintained in the system reflect the versions in force for the term being reviewed. Updates are made when applicable requirements change.

\end{itemize}


\section{Failure Mode and Effect Analysis}

% \wss{Include your FMEA table here. This is the most important part of this document.}
% \wss{The safety requirements in the table do not have to have the prefix SR.

This section applies Failure Mode and Effect Analysis (FMEA) to each component
identified in the System Boundaries and Components section (C1 to C11). For each component,
the team asked how it could fail, what loss each failure would lead to, why it
could happen, and how it would be noticed. Table~\ref{TblFMEA} records the
result. Each row gives:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Ref:} a unique identifier for the hazard, in the form
    H\textit{component}-\textit{row}, where \textit{component} is the
    component number (H4-2 is the second hazard of C4), followed by the hazard's category and
    severity (Tables~\ref{TblHazardCategories} and~\ref{TblHazardSeverity}).
    The safety and security requirements refer back to these identifiers.
  \item \textbf{Failure Mode, Effects of Failure, Causes of Failure:} what goes
    wrong, the loss it leads to, and why it could happen.
  \item \textbf{Recommended Action:} how the failure is prevented or its effect
    is limited.
  \item \textbf{Req.:} the requirement that implements each recommended action.
    Where a row has more than one action, the actions and their requirements
    are lettered a., b., and so on. FR, NFR, DR, AIR and A refer to the
    functional, nonfunctional, data and AI requirements and the assumptions in
    the SRS. SSR refers to the safety and security requirements in this
    document (Table~\ref{TblSafetySecurityRequirements}). ``New'' marks an action that does not yet have a requirement;
    these are listed after the table.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The analysis identified the following recommended actions that do not
yet have a requirement. Each will be added to the Safety and Security
Requirements (Table~\ref{TblSafetySecurityRequirements}) and to the SRS:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Warning ranking} (H8-2): rank workload clusters by size.
  \item \textbf{Session timeout} (H9-3): end a session after inactivity.
  \item \textbf{Model change test} (H11-2): re-run the accuracy test set before a
    model change is deployed.
  \item \textbf{Output validation} (H4-5): check model output against the
    expected structure.
  \item \textbf{Backup and restore} (H2-1): back up stored data and test
    restoring it.
  \item \textbf{Confirm before replace} (H2-2): keep confirmed values when an
    outline is uploaded again.
  \item \textbf{Reference version shown} (H5-4, H5-6, H6-5): record which
    calendar, master course sheet, and policy version each result used.
  \item \textbf{Export limits} (H10-1): export only confirmed data the user's
    role may see.
  \item \textbf{CSV escaping} (H10-2): escape exported cells that could run as
    formulas.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Row H4-6 also led to a change in the SRS: AIR1 now excludes the
master course sheet from the model's input, and the graduate attributes are
compared with the sheet by the system's own code.
