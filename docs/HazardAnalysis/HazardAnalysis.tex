\section{Critical Assumptions}

%\wss{These assumptions that are made about the software or system. You should
%minimize the number of assumptions that remove potential hazards. For instance,
%you could assume a part will never fail, but it is generally better to include
%this potential failure mode.}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Course Outline Quality:} Uploaded course outlines may contain incomplete, 
    inconsistent, or incorrectly formatted information that could affect the accuracy of extracted data.
    
    \item \textbf{Information Extraction:} The course outline processing component, including any LLM 
    services used, may incorrectly interpret or omit information such as marking schemes, assignment 
    deadlines, and midterm dates.
    
    \item \textbf{Policy and Accreditation Requirements:} The McMaster policies and CEAB requirements 
    used for compliance analysis must be relevant to the courses being reviewed. Outdated or incorrectly 
    interpreted requirements may lead to inaccurate compliance findings.
    
    \item \textbf{Deadline Information:} Assessment dates and deadlines extracted from course outlines may be 
    incomplete, ambiguous, or subject to change. As a result, the system may fail to properly detect actual deadline bottlenecks.
    
    \item \textbf{Database Integrity:} Stored course outline information and extracted data may become outdated 
    or associated with the wrong course.
    
    \item \textbf{External Dependencies:} External LLM services and software libraries may produce incorrect 
    results, become unavailable, or change their behavior (their outputs and availability cannot be guaranteed).
    
    \item \textbf{Human Review and Decision-Making:} CAS administrators and instructors are expected to review 
    the system's findings and recommendations. However, users may overlook or place excessive confidence in inaccurate system outputs.
    
    \item \textbf{Scheduling Recommendations:} While the system provides recommendations to help reduce deadline bottlenecks, 
    it cannot guarantee that all student workload conflicts will be eliminated (instructors retain responsibility for final scheduling decisions).
\end{itemize}
